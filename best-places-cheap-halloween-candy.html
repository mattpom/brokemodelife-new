Best Places to Find Inexpensive Halloween Candy

More treats. Less money. Compare the stores and the math before you fill your candy bowl.

October 5, 2026 · Broke Mode Life

Halloween candy should scare the neighborhood kids, not your grocery budget. The best place to buy it depends on how many visitors you expect, whether you already have a warehouse membership, and what kind of candy you want to hand out.

Start with Walmart or Target if you want a straightforward comparison without a warehouse membership. Check Sam’s Club or Costco if you already belong and expect a busy porch. Dollar Tree is worth checking for a small top-up, but a low bag price alone does not prove you are getting a bargain.

Researched October 5, 2026. Prices below are public online examples, not verified Indianapolis store quotes. Local stock, promotions, tax, membership costs, and fulfillment fees can change the total. These are places to compare—not a claim that one retailer is cheapest everywhere.

Where to look first

1. Sam’s Club: worth checking for a crowded porch

The Frankford Mega Mix was listed at $28.98 for more than 600 individually wrapped pieces. Using 600 pieces conservatively, that is about 4.8 cents each before additional costs.

That makes it an option to investigate for a large event or neighborhood crowd. But a mixed bag of small sweets is not the same purchase as a bag of chocolate bars. Check the assortment and portion sizes. If you do not already belong, include the membership cost before calling it a saving.

2. Walmart: a useful starting point without a club membership

Walmart’s candy category showed a 200-count M&M’s, Snickers, Twix and Milky Way chocolate mix at $24.87—about 12.4 cents per piece. It is a useful benchmark when comparing other chocolate assortments.

Check the selected bag size, seller, and pickup location. An online marketplace offer is not necessarily the price at your nearby store. Compare the final total rather than assuming every listing comes with the same delivery terms.

3. Target: compare smaller bags as well as bulk packs

Target’s Halloween variety selection showed a 60-count Mars chocolate-and-fruit candy bag at $9.99, or about 16.7 cents per piece. That can make more sense for a quiet porch than spending nearly $30 on candy you will not use.

Check eligible offers before paying. A promotion is useful only if it applies to the bags you want and the final price beats your alternative. Bigger packs and different varieties may have different costs per piece.

4. Costco: compare the warehouse with the website

Kirkland Signature Favorites is a 90-ounce, individually packed chocolate assortment. Its listed varieties include familiar brands, but the mix can vary.

If you already have a membership, check your warehouse price against other large chocolate bags. Do not assume a delivery or online price matches the shelf price. When a piece count is not stated, compare price per ounce against similar candy; do not invent a cost per piece from the bag’s weight.

5. Dollar Tree: check for small quantities and last-minute extras

Dollar Tree lists Halloween candy and snacks among its seasonal categories. It is a place to check if you need only a few extra treats or want to keep the amount spent today small.

Look at the package size and number of individually wrapped treats. Several small bags can cost more than one larger bag elsewhere. Confirm the actual shelf price rather than assuming every item has the same price.

Use the number that matches your plan

Cost per wrapped piece = bag price ÷ individually wrapped pieces. If you hand out two pieces per child, double that number. Compare similar portion sizes: a tiny chew, a mini chocolate, and a fun-size bar do not offer the same amount of candy.

For a party candy bowl, price per ounce can be more helpful. For trick-or-treating, count the sealed treats or treat-size packets you can actually hand out. A large bag of loose candy is not the same thing as individually wrapped portions.

Buy for your neighborhood, not the biggest display

Estimate visitors from last year or ask a neighbor. Multiply that estimate by the pieces you plan to give each child, then add a modest cushion. For example, 60 children receiving two pieces each need 120 pieces before that cushion. Treat this as a planning example, not a prediction of your turnout.

A low cost per piece does not help if you buy hundreds of unnecessary pieces. Split a large bag with a neighbor when that genuinely reduces both households’ costs. Buying a membership solely for one candy purchase needs a separate calculation.

Three mistakes that wipe out the savings

  • Comparing the price on the front of the bag. Check count, weight, and portion size before choosing.
  • Driving all over town for a tiny saving. Include fuel, time, shipping, and delivery fees. A store on your normal route may be the better purchase.
  • Relying on after-Halloween clearance for Halloween night. Clearance can be useful for later occasions, but it arrives after trick-or-treating and neither selection nor discounts are guaranteed.

The Broke Mode approach

Pick two convenient stores, compare similar wrapped treats, and buy enough for your expected crowd. For lots of visitors, check warehouse bulk mixes if you already have access. For fewer visitors, a smaller bag from Walmart or Target can keep your total spending lower even when the per-piece price is higher.

The goal is a full candy bowl and a budget that still works on November 1.
